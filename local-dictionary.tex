\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{Coefficient category: Tate $\C$-vector spaces.}{Coefficient category: Tate C-vector spaces.}}
The coefficient-side linear algebra takes place in the symmetric
monoidal category $\mathrm{Tate}_\C$ of Tate $\C$-vector spaces:
ind-pro finite-dimensional $\C$-vector spaces equipped with linearly
compact lattices.  A linearly compact module is a lattice object, not
the whole Tate object.  Here \emph{profinite-dimensional} means a
strict inverse limit of finite-dimensional \(\C\)-vector spaces with the
inverse-limit topology, and \emph{indfinite-dimensional} means a
filtered direct limit, equivalently a strict union, of finite-dimensional
\(\C\)-vector spaces with the final locally convex topology.  The
coefficient completed tensor product
$\widehat\otimes_{\mathrm{Tate}}$ is the topological tensor product on
$\mathrm{Tate}_\C$; continuous duality is the strict functor
$V\mapsto V^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}=\Hom^{\mathrm{cts}}_\C(V,\C)$.  When
Dolbeault, de Rham, heat-kernel, or local-functional factors are
present, their analytic tensor product is the nuclear projective tensor
product \(\widehat\otimes_\pi\); the symbol \(\widehat\otimes\) below
means the completed product obtained from
\(\widehat\otimes_\pi\) on analytic factors and
\(\widehat\otimes_{\mathrm{Tate}}\) on coefficient factors.  No
bornological tensor product is used unless explicitly named.  The
$\C$-algebra $A=\C[[z_1,z_2]]$ is a complete linearly topologized
$\C$-algebra with the $\mathfrak m$-adic filtration
$\mathfrak m^k=(z_1,z_2)^kA$; $\mathfrak h=A/\C\!\cdot\!1$ is
profinite-dimensional in this filtration, and its strict continuous
dual is the Grothendieck--Matlis principal-part module
\[
  \mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}
  =\mathcal P
  =\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathrm{res}}(\C\cdot1)\subset H^2_{\mathfrak m}(A)\otimes\omega_A,
  \qquad
  \mathcal P=\bigoplus_{a+b>0}\C\,\rho_{a,b},
  \quad
  \rho_{a,b}=z_1^{-a-1}z_2^{-b-1}\,dz_1\,dz_2,
\]
indfinite-dimensional, with the residue pairing
$\langle f,\omega\rangle=\operatorname{Res}_0\,f\omega$ a perfect pairing of Tate
$\C$-modules.  The finite Matlis window is
\(\mathcal P_{\le K}=\bigoplus_{a+b\le K}\C\rho_{a,b}\).  Two
transition conventions are kept separate.  The strict Matlis module
\(\mathcal P\) is the filtered colimit
\(\varinjlim_K\mathcal P_{\le K}\) along inclusions.  A pro-Matlis
inverse tower uses the coordinate truncations
\[
  \pi_{K+1,K}\colon \mathcal P_{\le K+1}\twoheadrightarrow
  \mathcal P_{\le K},
\]
and its derived inverse limit means the Roos complex
\(\operatorname{Roos}(\mathcal P_{\le \bullet},\pi_{\bullet,\bullet-1})\),
written \(\mathbf{R}\!\varprojlim_K\mathcal P_{\le K}\).  Thus
\(\mathbf{R}\!\varprojlim_K\mathcal P_{\le K}\) denotes the pro-Matlis
tower, not the direct-sum Matlis module, unless a separate
Mittag--Leffler identification is supplied; all \(\varprojlim^1\)-data
are tracked.  Polynomial calculations use the dense
indfinite-dimensional sub-Lie algebra $\bar A=\C[z_1,z_2]/\C\cdot 1$;
completed CE/PV calculations use $\mathfrak h$ itself.  Factorization
algebras throughout are taken in the Costello--Gwilliam sense
(precosheaf on opens, Weiss-cover descent), refined to mixed-HT opens
on $\R^2\times\C^2$.  This is the analytic incarnation of the
factorisable-$\mathcal D$-module-on-$\mathrm{Ran}(\C^2)$ description ---
the two-complex-dimensional extension of the
$\mathcal D$-module-on-Ran presentation of curve chiral algebras
formalised in \cite{beilinson-drinfeld-chiral}*{\S 3}, equivalent to the analytic
form via the de Rham complex; the BV / heat-kernel formalism selects
Costello--Gwilliam as native.

\begin{defn}[Block-sum primitive projection]\label{defn:block-sum-primitive-projection}
  Let \(\mathcal H_N\) be a family of graded commutative
  \(GL_N\)-invariant trace algebras, with block-diagonal restriction
  maps
  \[
    \Delta^\oplus_{N_1,N_2}\colon
    \mathcal H_{N_1+N_2}
    \longrightarrow
    \mathcal H_{N_1}\otimes\mathcal H_{N_2}
  \]
  induced by
  \(\mathfrak{gl}_{N_1}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{N_2}
  \hookrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}_{N_1+N_2}\).  On a single trace,
  \[
    \Delta^\oplus_{N_1,N_2}\operatorname{Tr}_{N_1+N_2}(w)
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}_{N_1}(w)\otimes 1
    +
    1\otimes\operatorname{Tr}_{N_2}(w),
  \]
  and the formula extends multiplicatively.  In a fixed word degree
  \(d\), after passing to the stable range \(N\ge d\), the maps
  \(\Delta^\oplus_{N_1,N_2}\) define the cocommutative Hopf algebra
  structure used in the trace-sector theorem.

  A compatible stable degree-\(d\) class
  \(x=(x_N)_{N\ge d}\) is \emph{block-sum primitive} if
  \[
    \Delta^\oplus_{N_1,N_2}(x_{N_1+N_2})
    =
    x_{N_1}\otimes 1+1\otimes x_{N_2}
  \]
  for all \(N_1,N_2\) in the same stable degree window.  The resulting
  primitive subspace is denoted \(\operatorname{Prim}_{\oplus}\).  If
  \(I\) is the augmentation ideal of the stable connected Hopf algebra,
  the connected/indecomposable projection is the quotient
  \[
    \operatorname{Conn}_{\oplus}\colon
    I\longrightarrow I/I^2 .
  \]
  For the stable polynomial trace Hopf algebra, this quotient is the
  single-trace generator space and agrees with the primitive subspace;
  decomposable products of nonempty traces lie in \(I^2\).  The empty
  trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_N(1)=N\) is removed before applying
  \(\operatorname{Prim}_{\oplus}\) or
  \(\operatorname{Conn}_{\oplus}\).

  In this manuscript
  \[
    \mathcal H_N=
    H^0\bigl((\mathcal R_N)^{GL_N},Q\bigr),
    \qquad Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2],
  \]
  and \(\operatorname{Prim}_{\mathrm{st}}\),
  \(\operatorname{Conn}_{\mathrm{st}}\), and
  \(\operatorname{Prim}_{\oplus}\) all refer to this stable
  block-sum Hopf structure.  They do not denote physical \(N\to\infty\)
  cumulants; those require a separate state.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Admissible host category datum]\label{defn:admissible-host-category}
  The symbol \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) used in the formal-stalk
  theorem denotes a fixed admissible host-category datum.  It is not a
  universal category of all filtered topological complexes.  Its objects
  are tuples
  \[
    (V,F^\bullet V,d,\widehat\otimes,V^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}},
    D_V,\mathrm{ev}_V,\mathrm{coev}_V,
    \underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^\bullet_{\mathrm{adm}},
    \operatorname{Roos},\,L_{\mathrm{sc}},
    \mathcal O_{\mathrm{adm}},\mathcal K_{\mathrm{adm}})
  \]
  where \((V,F^\bullet V,d)\) is a complete Hausdorff filtered cochain
  complex whose coefficient factor lies in \(\mathrm{Tate}_\C\) and
  whose analytic form or local-functional factors are nuclear
  locally convex complexes, \(V^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}}\) is the strict
  continuous dual, \(D_V\) is the declared coefficient dual used by BV
  pairings and kernels, \(L_{\mathrm{sc}}\) is the declared scalar line
  when one is present, and \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is the
  list of products, brackets, convolutions, trace transitions, and
  counterterm operations that are required to be continuous on \(V\), and
  \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{adm}}\) is the list of diagonal kernels,
  Casimirs, propagators, and local-cohomology kernels required to exist
  as elements of the declared completed tensor products or internal Hom
  complexes.  The symbol is not a free parameter: a theorem using an
  infinite diagonal Casimir, base heat-kernel homotopy, BV propagator
  datum, or restricted \(P_0\)-kernel must name a concrete object of this
  datum.  In this manuscript the role is played by the weighted Tate
  envelope
  \(B_w\in\mathsf{Adm}_w\) of
  Definition~\ref{defn:wt-Bw-envelope} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:verified-badm-weighted-model}, with
  \(w(d)=q^d\), \(q>1\).  The declared BV receiver
  \(D_{B_w}=D^{\mathrm{BV}}_w\) is the product
  coefficient module carrying the BV coevaluation; it is not the strict
  continuous dual of the Tate product \(\mathfrak h_w\).  Thus an object of
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) is a complex together with its
  completed tensor product, internal Hom, strict and declared duality,
  Roos/strict-limit convention, scalar convention, operation estimates,
  and admissible kernel list.  It is not an unspecified background
  category.  The data satisfy the following finite list of conditions.
  \begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(A\arabic*)}]
    \item \emph{Strict topology.}  The filtration is exhaustive and
      separated; differentials are strict continuous maps; cones and
      shifts carry the completed induced filtration.
    \item \emph{Completed tensor product.}  The monoidal product is the
      completed product \(\widehat\otimes\) obtained by applying the
      nuclear projective tensor product to analytic factors and the
      completed Tate tensor product to coefficient factors, then
      completing the algebraic tensor product for the convolution
      filtration
      \[
        F_n(V\widehat\otimes W)=
        \widehat{\sum_{i+j\leq n}\operatorname{im}
        (F_iV\otimes F_jW\to V\otimes W)}
      \]
      on the declared objects.  It preserves strict filtered
      quasi-isomorphisms in each variable.
    \item \emph{Continuous and declared duality.}  The strict continuous
      dual is
      \(V^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}}=\operatorname{Hom}^{\mathrm{cts}}_\C(V,\C)\)
      with the opposite Tate filtration.  The BV pairing in a theorem is
      carried by the declared coefficient dual \(D_V\), with continuous
      maps
      \[
        \mathrm{ev}_V\colon D_V\widehat\otimes V\to\C,\qquad
        \mathrm{coev}_V\colon \C\to V\widehat\otimes D_V .
      \]
      If \(D_V\ne V^\vee_{\mathrm{cts}}\), the theorem must state this
      coefficient enlargement and the topology in which
      \(\mathrm{coev}_V(1)\) converges.  No statement about \(D_V\) is
      then a statement about the strict continuous dual.
    \item \emph{Internal Hom.}  The dg Hom complex is
      \[
        \underline{\mathrm{Hom}}^k_{\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}}(V,W)
        =
        \{\,f\colon V\to W[k]\mid f
        \text{ is continuous, filtered, and preserves the declared
        admissibility data}\,\},
      \]
      with differential
      \(d_{\mathrm{Hom}}f=d_W f-(-1)^k f d_V\).  The internal Hom
      used in convolution and centrality complexes is the closed
      subcomplex consisting of maps that preserve scalar lines, pairings,
      stable transition maps, all operations listed in
      \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and all kernels listed in
      \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{adm}}\).
    \item \emph{Limits.}  Inverse systems are evaluated by the Roos
      complex \(\operatorname{Roos}(V_\bullet)\), hence by
      \(\mathbf R\!\varprojlim V_n\).  A strict inverse limit may be used
      only when the Mittag--Leffler term
      \(\varprojlim\nolimits^1 H^{*-1}(V_n)\) is zero; otherwise that
      \(\varprojlim^1\)-class is an obstruction datum, not discarded.
    \item\label{adm:scalar-reduction-functor}
      \emph{Scalar reduction.}  Let
      \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{sc}}\) be the dg category of
      pairs \((V,L)\) where \(V\in\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) and
      \(L\simeq\C\) is a declared split central dg subobject.  For an
      algebra object the source condition is stronger: \(L\) is a
      central dg ideal for the operation being quotiented.  The
      scalar-reduction functor is the exact quotient functor
      \[
        U_{\mathrm{sc}}\colon
        \mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}_{\mathrm{sc}}\longrightarrow
        \mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}},
        \qquad
        (V,L)\longmapsto \operatorname{coker}(L\hookrightarrow V).
      \]
      On a morphism \(f\colon(V,L)\to(W,M)\) satisfying
      \(f(L)\subset M\), \(U_{\mathrm{sc}}(f)\) is the induced map on
      cokernels.  The unit of an algebra is retained unless the
      theorem explicitly passes to Hamiltonian primitives.  This is the
      entire meaning of \(U_{\mathrm{sc}}\): it does not forget
      pro-Matlis completions, replace Roos limits by strict limits,
      discard Hochschild centrality homotopies, pass to stable traces,
      or erase a QME counterterm tower.

      In the Dirac-brane trace construction, Hamiltonian scalar
      reduction is not an application of \(U_{\mathrm{sc}}\) to either
      the algebra unit or the class \(\operatorname{Tr}\psi\).  It is the
      replacement
      \[
        C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\left(\mathfrak{gl}_N,
          \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N^{\oplus2}
          \oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N[1])\right)
        \longmapsto
        C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\left(\mathfrak{pgl}_N,
          \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N^{\oplus2}
          \oplus\mathfrak{sl}_N[1])\right).
      \]
      The empty trace is then omitted from the primitive generator
      space before a new algebra unit is adjoined by the completed
      symmetric-algebra functor.  The equation \(K=0\) for the
      projective central extension is a separate central reduction; it
      neither produces the effective \(\mathfrak{pgl}_N/\mathfrak{sl}_N\)
      Koszul model nor removes the empty trace.
    \item\label{adm:stable-trace-passage-functor}
      \emph{Stable trace passage.}  Fix a word degree \(d\).  Let
      \(\mathrm{Tow}^{\mathrm{tr}}_d(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}})\) be the
      category whose objects are finite-window rational towers
      \[
        \bigl(E_{N,d},\,r_{N+1,N}\bigr)_{N\geq d},
        \qquad
        \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}}
        :=
        \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N^{\oplus2}
          \oplus\mathfrak{sl}_N[1]),
      \]
      \[
        \mathcal H_{N,+}^{\mathrm{eff}}
        :=
        \ker\!\left(
          H^0\bigl((\mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}})^{GL_N},Q\bigr)
          \longrightarrow\C\right),
        \qquad
        E_{N,d}:=
        \left(\operatorname{Prim}_{\oplus}
          \mathcal H_{N,+}^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)_d,
      \]
      where each \(E_{N,d}\) is placed in degree zero as a discrete
      finite-dimensional Tate object and \(r_{N+1,N}\) is the
      transition map induced by the Procesi--Razmyslov stable trace
      identification in degree \(d\).  A morphism is a compatible tower
      of continuous maps in \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).  The
      primitive operation is
      Definition~\ref{defn:block-sum-primitive-projection}.  The degree
      \(d\) stable trace functor is
      \[
        \operatorname{StTr}_d\colon
        \mathrm{Tow}^{\mathrm{tr}}_d(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}})
        \longrightarrow
        \mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}},
        \qquad
        (E_{N,d})_{N\ge d}\longmapsto
        \operatorname{Roos}(E_{\bullet,d}).
      \]
      If the tower is Mittag--Leffler, this Roos complex is represented
      by the strict inverse limit
      \[
        \varprojlim_{N\geq d}
        \left(\operatorname{Prim}_{\oplus}
          \mathcal H_{N,+}^{\mathrm{eff}}\right)_d;
      \]
      otherwise the \(\varprojlim^1\)-class is part of the obstruction
      datum and is not discarded.  The completed functor
      \[
        \operatorname{StTr}
        =
        \widehat{\bigoplus}_{d\ge1}\operatorname{StTr}_d
        \colon
        \prod_{d\ge1}
        \mathrm{Tow}^{\mathrm{tr}}_d(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}})
        \longrightarrow
        \mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}
      \]
      lands in the declared Tate completion chosen for the theorem using
      it.  In the formal-Darboux theorem this output is identified with
      the reduced Hamiltonian generator space \(\mathfrak h\), and
      \[
        A_{\partial,H}^{\mathrm{st}}
        =
        \widehat{\mathbf S}_{\mathrm{Tate}}
        \!\left(\widehat{\bigoplus}_{d\geq1}
          \operatorname{StTr}_d(E_{\bullet,d})\right)
      \]
      is the completed symmetric algebra on that output.  Its unit is
      the newly adjoined symmetric-algebra unit, not the empty matrix
      trace \(\operatorname{Tr}_N(1)=N\), which was absent from
      \(\mathcal H_{N,+}^{\mathrm{eff}}\) before the primitive
      projection.  In the declared source range \(N\ge d\),
      the Procesi--Razmyslov transition maps identify the degree-\(d\)
      primitive trace representatives, so the representative map to
      \(\operatorname{StTr}_d(E_{\bullet,d})\) has no additional
      degree-\(d\) trace-identity kernel.  Trace identities for smaller
      ranks, and positive Chevalley--Eilenberg-length stabilizer classes
      of the quotient stack, are not in the source of
      \(\operatorname{StTr}_d\).  Here
      \(H^0((\mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}})^{GL_N},Q)\) means the
      Chevalley--Eilenberg-length-zero invariant Koszul cohomology of
      the effective polynomial brane complex.  Since the scalar centre
      acts trivially on this coefficient complex, these invariants equal
      the \(\mathrm{PGL}_N\)-invariants; the \(GL_N\) notation retains
      the block-sum transition maps.  Reductive exactness is the
      finite-window rational-module statement of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:reductive-invariants-koszul-exactness}.  Physical
      \(N\to\infty\) trace-state data and cumulants are separate data, not
      the functor \(\operatorname{StTr}\).
    \item \emph{Operation estimates.}  Every product, bracket,
      coadjoint action, Hochschild convolution, and Costello counterterm
      tower acting on an object of
      \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) is accompanied by a continuity
      estimate in the chosen filtration.  For the Matlis cotangent
      action this means either the locally finite direct-sum statement of
      Lemma~\ref{lem:app-matlis-coadjoint-continuity}, where a fixed
      principal part sees only a finite jet of a formal Hamiltonian, or
      the pro-Matlis seminorm estimate of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:completion-promatlis-continuity}.  A finite
      Matlis cutoff is not a strict \(\mathfrak h\)-submodule unless the
      window-loss term is recorded.
    \item \emph{Kernel admissibility.}  A theorem invoking a diagonal
      Casimir, a diagonal local-cohomology kernel, a heat-kernel
      propagator, or a Maurer--Cartan kernel must include that kernel in
      \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{adm}}\).  This means a compatible finite-window
      family of tensors has a limit
      \[
        K\in V\widehat\otimes D_V
        \quad\text{or}\quad
        K\in
        \underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}}^{\bullet}
        (C,V)
      \]
      in the declared topology, with evaluation and coevaluation maps
      continuous for that same topology.  The strict product/direct-sum
      Tate pair may supply evaluation; it is not kernel-admissible for
      the BV diagonal unless the coevaluation tensor converges.
    \item \emph{Verification scope.}  A statement made in
      \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) specifies the object, its operation
      list \(\mathcal O_{\mathrm{adm}}\), its kernel list
      \(\mathcal K_{\mathrm{adm}}\), and the estimates or cited
      propositions verifying (A1)--(A10).  In this manuscript the
      finite-window Hamiltonian model is
      Proposition~\ref{prop:verified-badm-finite-model}; the infinite
      diagonal model is the weighted object
      \(B_w\in\mathsf{Adm}_w\) of
      Proposition~\ref{prop:verified-badm-weighted-model}.  Centre lifts,
      descent, all-order QME counterterms, anomaly homotopies, locality
      primitives, and open--closed cone contractions are separate
      obstruction data named in the theorems that use them.  The word
      ``admissible'' supplies the host-category continuity,
      completion, kernel, and limit conditions above.
  \end{enumerate}
  Morphisms in \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) are degree-zero closed
  elements of the dg Hom complex.  Weak equivalences are strict
  filtered quasi-isomorphisms, or filtered quasi-isomorphisms after
  replacing the inverse system by its Roos complex.
\end{defn}

\begin{rmk}[Model objects and non-objects in \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\)]
\label{rmk:badm-models}
The finite-window object \(B_{\mathrm{fin}}\) is admissible: all sums
are finite, the tensor product is algebraic before completion, the
residue pairing is finite-dimensional, and the Schouten bracket,
Koszul differential, and trace maps are strict continuous maps.

For \(q>1\), let \(w(d)=q^d\).  The weighted Tate envelope
\(B_w\) of Definition~\ref{defn:wt-Bw-envelope} is admissible by
Theorem~\ref{thm:wt-canonical-envelope}.  Its declared coefficient
dual is
\(D^{\mathrm{BV}}_w\) of
Definition~\ref{defn:wt-coefficient-pair}; this is the product
coefficient module that carries the diagonal BV coevaluation.  It is
not the strict continuous dual of the Tate product
\(\mathfrak h_w\), whose strict continuous dual is the direct sum.
The product, Schouten bracket, Schouten differential, coadjoint
action, weighted diagonal Casimir, supplied heat-kernel BV propagator
datum, fixed-graph renormalization-group contribution, and CE/PV map are
the operations listed in Theorem~\ref{thm:wt-canonical-envelope}.

The strict Tate product/direct-sum pair is excluded by
Proposition~\ref{prop:badm-strict-product-direct-sum-nonobject}: it has
evaluation but no coevaluation kernel for the BV propagator used in the
formal-stalk theorem.
\end{rmk}

\begin{prop}[Strict product/direct-sum non-object]\label{prop:badm-strict-product-direct-sum-nonobject}
  Let
  \[
    H_d=\operatorname{Span}_{\C}\{z_1^az_2^b:a+b=d\},\qquad
    \mathfrak h_\Pi=\prod_{d\ge1}H_d,\qquad
    D_\oplus=\bigoplus_{d\ge1}H_d^\vee .
  \]
  The pair \((\mathfrak h_\Pi,D_\oplus)\) has a continuous evaluation
  pairing \(D_\oplus\widehat\otimes\mathfrak h_\Pi\to\C\), but it is
  not an object of \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) carrying the BV
  coevaluation required by the formal-stalk theorem.  The diagonal
  coevaluation
  \[
    C_\Pi=\sum_{d\ge1}\sum_i e_{d,i}\otimes e^i_d
  \]
  lies in neither
  \(\mathfrak h_\Pi\widehat\otimes D_\oplus\) nor
  \(D_\oplus\widehat\otimes\mathfrak h_\Pi\).
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  An element of a completed tensor product with the direct-sum factor
  \(D_\oplus=\bigoplus_{d\ge1}H_d^\vee\) has finite support in the
  \(D_\oplus\)-degree direction in the strict Tate convention used
  here.  The displayed diagonal has nonzero \(H_d^\vee\)-component for
  every \(d\ge1\).  Hence it is not a tensor in
  \(\mathfrak h_\Pi\widehat\otimes D_\oplus\).  The same support
  obstruction holds after reversing the tensor factors.

  Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category}(A3) requires a
  continuous coevaluation \(\C\to V\widehat\otimes D_V\) for every BV
  pairing used by a theorem.  The strict product/direct-sum pair
  supplies evaluation, while the inverse kernel is absent.  Therefore a
  theorem using the diagonal BV propagator on this pair has no
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\)-object as its coefficient source.  The
  weighted Tate model of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:verified-badm-weighted-model} changes the
  coefficient category precisely to supply this coevaluation.
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[Verified admissible finite Hamiltonian model]
\label{prop:verified-badm-finite-model}
  Let
  \[
    H_{\leq 2}
    =
    \operatorname{Span}_{\C}\{z_1,z_2,z_1^2,z_1z_2,z_2^2\}
    \subset \C[z_1,z_2]/\C\cdot1 .
  \]
  Since \(\{H_{\leq2},H_{\leq2}\}\subset H_{\leq2}\) modulo constants,
  \(H_{\leq2}\) is a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian Lie algebra.  Put
  \(P_{\leq2}=H_{\leq2}^{\vee}\) with the coadjoint action dual to this
  finite Lie bracket, and
  \(\mathfrak g_{\leq2}=H_{\leq2}\ltimes P_{\leq2}[1]\).  Put
  \[
    B_{\leq2}
    =
    \widehat{\mathbf S}(H_{\leq2})
    \widehat\otimes
    \Lambda(H_{\leq2}^{\vee})
  \]
  and equip it with \(I\)-adic filtration \(F^rB_{\leq2}=I^r\), where
  \(I=(O_f,\partial^\lambda)\), differential
  \(d_\pi=[\frac12C^K_{IJ}O_K\partial^I\partial^J,-]_S\), ordinary completed
  tensor product over finite-dimensional coefficient spaces, continuous
  dual \(B_{\leq2}^{\vee,\mathrm{cont}}\), Roos convention equal to the
  constant inverse system, no scalar line \(L_{\mathrm{sc}}=0\), and
  operation list
  \[
    \mathcal O_{\leq2}
    =
    \{\text{multiplication},\,d_\pi,\,[\, ,\,]_S,\,
      H_{\leq2}\text{-coadjoint action}\}
  \]
  and kernel list
  \[
    \mathcal K_{\leq2}=\{\Delta_{\leq2}\}
  \]
  where \(\Delta_{\leq2}=\sum_i e_i\otimes e^i\) for dual bases
  \(\{e_i\}\) of \(H_{\leq2}\) and \(\{e^i\}\) of \(P_{\leq2}\).  This
  tuple is an object of \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  The Poisson bracket of two generators of degree at most two has degree
  at most two, and the only degree-zero bracket is a constant, killed in
  \(\C[z_1,z_2]/\C\cdot1\).  Hence \(H_{\leq2}\) is a finite Lie algebra,
  and \(P_{\leq2}=H_{\leq2}^{\vee}\) is its ordinary finite coadjoint
  module.

  Conditions (A1)--(A10) of
  Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category} are then checked inside
  finite-dimensional linear algebra.  For (A1), the \(I\)-adic completion
  is
  \[
    B_{\leq2}=\varprojlim_r B_{\leq2}/I^r,
  \]
  with finite-dimensional quotients; it is complete, Hausdorff, and
  separated.  Since \(d_\pi(O_K)=C^L_{KJ}O_L\partial^J\) and
  \(d_\pi(\partial^K)=-\frac12C^K_{IJ}\partial^I\partial^J\),
  \(d_\pi(I^r)\subset I^{r+1}\), so the differential is strict
  continuous.  For (A2), the tensor product is the \(I\)-adically
  completed tensor product of finite-dimensional quotients; over \(\C\)
  it preserves strict filtered quasi-isomorphisms.  For (A3), the
  continuous dual is the direct sum of the duals of the homogeneous
  finite-dimensional quotients, and the pairing with \(B_{\leq2}\) is
  degreewise perfect.  The finite diagonal
  \[
    \Delta_{\leq2}=\sum_i e_i\otimes e^i
    \in H_{\leq2}\otimes P_{\leq2}
  \]
  is an honest tensor, so no convergence hypothesis is hidden.

  For (A4), the internal Hom is the complex of continuous filtered maps
  preserving \(\mathcal O_{\leq2}\), with differential
  \(d_{\mathrm{Hom}}f=d f-(-1)^{|f|}f d\); preservation of a finite list
  of continuous operations cuts out a closed subcomplex.  For (A5), the
  inverse system is constant, so the Roos complex is the object itself
  and \(\varprojlim^1=0\).  For (A6), no Capelli or empty-trace scalar
  line is declared; the scalar-reduction functor is the identity and the
  unit of \(\widehat{\mathbf S}(H_{\leq2})\) is retained.  For (A7), no
  stable \(N\)-trace passage is part of \(\mathcal O_{\leq2}\); finite
  invariant trace towers are therefore not invoked.

  For (A8), all operation estimates are explicit in the \(I\)-adic
  filtration:
  \[
    I^rI^s\subset I^{r+s},\qquad
    d_\pi(I^r)\subset I^{r+1},\qquad
    [I^r,I^s]_S\subset I^{r+s-1}.
  \]
  The coadjoint action uses only the finite pair
  \(H_{\leq2}\otimes P_{\leq2}\).  Thus every operation in
  \(\mathcal O_{\leq2}\) is continuous in the declared topology.  For
  (A9), the finite Casimir
  \(\Delta_{\leq2}\in\mathcal K_{\leq2}\) is an actual tensor in
  \(H_{\leq2}\otimes P_{\leq2}\), hence supplies the required
  coevaluation kernel.  For (A10), the proposition has specified the
  object, operation list, kernel list, and finite-dimensional estimates.
  Therefore \(B_{\leq2}\) is a verified object of
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).
\end{proof}

\begin{prop}[Verified admissible weighted Hamiltonian model]
\label{prop:verified-badm-weighted-model}

  \((\mathfrak h_w,D^{\mathrm{BV}}_w)\) be the

  \]
  and kernel list
  \[
    \mathcal K_w=\{C_{\mathfrak h,w},\,P^w_{\epsilon,L}\}.
  \]
  Here \(C_{\mathfrak h,w}\) is the weighted diagonal Casimir,
  \(P^w_{\epsilon,L}\) is the weighted heat-kernel propagator, \(\Gamma\)

  vertices, and \(\Phi^w\) is the weighted CE/PV map.  This tuple is an
  object of \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  Lemma~\ref{lem:wt-weights-exist} verifies the degree-weight
  conditions for \(w(d)=q^d\).  The tower of finite degree windows is
  strict Mittag--Leffler by Definition~\ref{defn:wt-degree-weight},
  hence inverse limits are computed by the Roos complex and have no
  hidden \(\varprojlim^1\)-term in the coevaluation tower.  The completed
  tensor product and declared BV receiver are those of
  Definition~\ref{defn:wt-coefficient-pair}; the strict continuous dual
  \(B_w^{\vee,\mathrm{cts}}\) remains separate from \(D_{B_w}\), as
  required by Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category}.

  Theorem~\ref{thm:wt-canonical-envelope} proves closure of \(B_w\)
  under multiplication, \(d_\pi\), and the Schouten bracket; convergence
  of the weighted diagonal as the declared coevaluation kernel
  \[
    C_{\mathfrak h,w}\in
    \mathfrak h_w\widehat\otimes D_{B_w}
    \subset B_w\widehat\otimes D_{B_w},
    \qquad D_{B_w}=D^{\mathrm{BV}}_w;
  \]
  this is not a tensor in \(B_w\widehat\otimes B_w\) unless the declared
  BV coefficient partner has separately been identified with \(B_w\).
  The same theorem gives extension of
  \(P^w_{\epsilon,L}\) as a continuous heat-kernel BV propagator in this
  declared kernel pair;
  fixed-graph renormalization-group stability; and continuity of the
  cochain map \(\Phi^w\).  These give the operation estimates required

  condition (A9).  No scalar quotient or stable trace passage is part of
  this model, so (A6) and (A7) are identities.  The proposition itself
  supplies the verification scope required in (A10).  Thus the data

  Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category}.
\end{proof}

\begin{defn}[Dirac brane stack and boundary types]\label{defn:dirac-brane-stack-boundaries}
  Fix \(p\in\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\) and let
  \[
    L=\R_t\times\{0_s\}\times\{p\}\subset
    X=\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}
  \]
  be the worldline defect.  A Dirac brane at \(p\) is the formal
  skyscraper boundary condition supported on \(L\).  A stack of \(N\)
  Dirac branes means this boundary condition with Chan--Paton space
  \(\C^N\).  In a formal Darboux chart
  \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{\C^2,p}\cong\C[[z_1,z_2]]\), the transverse
  coordinates act by
  \(z_i\mapsto\phi_i\in\mathfrak{gl}_N\), and the classical open
  observable chart is the quotient-stack Koszul cdga
  \[
    A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}
    =
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\left(\mathfrak{gl}_N,\,
      \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N
      \oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N[1])\right),
    \qquad
    Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2].
	  \]
	  Here \(\mathrm{GL}_N\) acts by simultaneous conjugation on
	  \(\phi_1,\phi_2,\psi\), and the Chevalley--Eilenberg factor is the
	  quotient-stack cochain model.
	  Equivalently, its transverse derived moduli chart is the derived
	  commuting stack
  \[
    [\mu^{-1}_{\mathrm{der}}(0)/\mathrm{GL}_N],
    \qquad
    \mu(\phi_1,\phi_2)=[\phi_1,\phi_2].
  \]
  This definition is local and formal.  It is not the compact brane
  moduli problem and not the cyclic framed Hilbert-scheme branch.

  The Costello--Gwilliam brane-link boundary used in the local
  open--closed comparison is
  \[
    \partial_{\mathrm{link}}X
    =
    \partial(X\setminus\nu(L)),
  \]
  the boundary of an excised tubular neighbourhood of \(L\); in the
  local model it is the sphere bundle \(L\times S^4_\epsilon\).  The
  Kato--Nakayama boundary \(\partial X^{\mathrm{KN}}\) is a different
  global object: it is attached to a chosen log compactification
  \((\overline X,D)\).  A comparison between
  \(\partial_{\mathrm{link}}X\) and \(\partial X^{\mathrm{KN}}\) is
  extra matched-conventions data and is not part of the local
  formal-Darboux theorem.  In local brane-link constructions below,
  \(\partial X\) denotes \(\partial_{\mathrm{link}}X\); the
  Kato--Nakayama boundary is always written as \(\partial
  X^{\mathrm{KN}}\).
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Formal-Darboux stalk functor]\label{defn:formal-darboux-stalk-functor}
  Let
  \(X=\R^k_{\mathrm{top}}\times Y_{\mathrm{hol}}\), where
  \(Y\) is holomorphic symplectic of complex dimension \(d=2m\) with
  symplectic form \(\omega_Y\), and let \(p\in Y\).  The
  formal-Darboux stalk functor is the symmetric monoidal restriction
  functor
  \[
    \operatorname{FDSt}_p\colon
    \mathrm{ChirAlg}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{k,d}(X)
    \longrightarrow
    \mathrm{ChirAlg}^{\mathrm{HT}}_{k,d}
    (\R^k_{\mathrm{top}}\times\widehat{Y}_p),
    \qquad
    \mathcal F\longmapsto
    \mathcal F|_{\R^k_{\mathrm{top}}\times\widehat{Y}_p}.
  \]
  The target uses
  \(\widehat{Y}_p=\operatorname{Spf}\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Y,p}\)
  together with a choice
  \[
    \chi\colon
    \bigl(\widehat{\mathbb A}^{2m}_0,\sum_{i=1}^{m}dq_i\wedge dp_i\bigr)
    \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}
    (\widehat Y_p,\omega_Y|_{\widehat Y_p})
  \]
  of formal Darboux chart.  The set
  \(\mathscr D_p(Y,\omega_Y)\) of such charts is a torsor for
  \(\mathrm{Symp}^{\mathrm{form}}
  (\widehat{\mathbb A}^{2m}_0,\sum_i dq_i\wedge dp_i)\) by
  precomposition; equivalently, after a chart has been chosen, a second
  chart differs by a formal symplectomorphism of the completed disk.
  Thus the restriction functor is intrinsic only after quotienting by
  this Darboux-chart torsor.  The displayed coordinate CE/PV algebra
  depends on the chosen \(\chi\): a change of chart acts by pullback on
  \(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{Y,p}\) and by the induced transport on the
  generators
  \(c^\rho,u_f,\partial_\rho,O_f=J(f),X_f=d_\pi O_f\), with
  \(\rho\in\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}\) and
  \(f\in\mathfrak h\).  Without the holomorphic
  symplectic form and even complex dimension, this construction is only
  a formal-completion stalk, not the Darboux stalk used in the local
  theorem.

  For the Hamiltonian BF bulk in this manuscript,
  \[
    \operatorname{FDSt}_p(\mathcal A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\mathrm{bulk}})
    =
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE},\mathrm{cont}}\!
    \left(\mathfrak h\ltimes\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}[1]\right),
    \qquad
    \mathfrak h=\C[[z_1,z_2]]/\C\cdot1.
  \]
  At \(N\) Dirac branes the boundary chart further applies
  \(z_i\mapsto\phi_i\in\mathfrak{gl}_N\) and the Koszul differential
  \(Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2]\).  The trace-sector theorem computes the
  image of this functor after passage to the effective
  \(\mathfrak{pgl}_N/\mathfrak{sl}_N\) model, omission of the empty
  primitive trace, stable trace passage, and admissible completion.  The
  full construction and equivariance
  proof are Definition~\ref{app:defn:Darboux-stalk-functor} and
  Proposition~\ref{app:prop:Darboux-equivariance}.
\end{defn}

\begin{defn}[Typed local source and target dictionary]
\label{defn:typed-local-source-target-dictionary}
The local theorem uses the following source and target objects.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item \(\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}_\partial\) is the primitive boundary
    open factorisation category on the brane-link boundary.  A vacuum
    \(b\in\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}_\partial\) gives the boundary
    \(E_1\)-algebra
    \[
      A_b=\End_{\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}_\partial}(b).
    \]
    An unadorned \(A_\partial\), when used in a theorem-specific
    section, denotes such a chosen boundary \(E_1\)-algebra; the local
    main theorem uses the typed symbols \(A_b\) and
    \(A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}\).
  \item The formal coordinate algebra at \(b\) is
    \[
      A_b^{\mathrm{coord}}
      =
      \widehat{\mathcal O}_{\C^2,b}
      \cong \C[[z_1,z_2]].
    \]
    It contains Hamiltonians on the Darboux normal disk.  It is not the
    boundary algebra whose Hochschild cochains are computed.
  \item The Hamiltonian source is
    \[
      \mathfrak h=A_b^{\mathrm{coord}}/\C\cdot1,\qquad
      \bar A=\C[z_1,z_2]/\C\cdot1,\qquad
      P=\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}=\mathcal P,\qquad
      \mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\ltimes P[1].
    \]
    Here \(\bar A\) is the dense polynomial Hamiltonian subalgebra used
    for finite trace and sign calculations; completed
    Chevalley--Eilenberg/polyvector calculations use \(\mathfrak h\).
  \item The \(\mathfrak h\)-action on \(P\) is the residue coadjoint
    action, not the Matlis \(R\)-module multiplication:
    \[
      f\cdot\rho=\pi_{\mathrm{pp}}\{f,\rho\},\qquad
      z_1^p z_2^q\cdot\rho_{a,b}
      =-(pb-qa+p-q)\rho_{a-p+1,b-q+1},
    \]
    with negative-index targets zero.  This is the action used in the
    semidirect product \(\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\ltimes P[1]\).  It
    extends continuously to
    \[
      \mathfrak h_{\mathfrak m\text{-adic}}\times
      \mathcal P_{\oplus}\longrightarrow\mathcal P_{\oplus},
    \]
    where \(\mathcal P_{\oplus}\) has the locally finite direct-sum
    topology: a fixed principal part sees only a finite jet of a formal
    Hamiltonian.  In the weighted pro-Matlis model the stronger
    estimate
    \[
      p^*_n(f\cdot\rho)\leq C_n\,p_{n+r}(f)\,p^*_{n+r}(\rho)
    \]
    is the continuity datum used by \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).
    These statements are
    Proposition~\ref{prop:app-matlis-coadjoint-action},
    Lemma~\ref{lem:app-matlis-coadjoint-continuity}, and
    Proposition~\ref{prop:completion-promatlis-continuity}.
  \item At a stack of \(N\) Dirac branes, the non-effective finite-rank
    boundary algebra is
    \[
      \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{neff}}
      =
      \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N
      \oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N[1]),
      \qquad
      A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}
      =
      C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\left(\mathfrak{gl}_N,\,
        \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{neff}}\right),
      \qquad Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2].
    \]
    Since \([\phi_1,\phi_2]\in\mathfrak{sl}_N\), the effective
    Hamiltonian Koszul model is
    \[
      \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}}
      =
      \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N
      \oplus\mathfrak{sl}_N[1]),
      \qquad
      A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N}
      =
      C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}(\mathfrak{pgl}_N,\mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}}).
    \]
    The central class \(\operatorname{Tr}\psi\) is the excess of the
    non-effective presentation.  It is not a stable Hamiltonian trace
    coordinate.  The augmentation ideal of \(A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}\) is
    \(\bar A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}\).  Fixed finite \(N\) retains
    trace relations, stabilizer cohomology, and unreduced centrality
    data.
  \item The stable scalar-reduced Hamiltonian trace algebra is
    \[
      A_{\partial,H}^{\mathrm{st}}
      :=
      \widehat{\mathbf S}_{\mathrm{Tate}}
      \!\left(\widehat{\bigoplus}_{d\geq1}
        \operatorname{StTr}_d(E_{\bullet,d})\right)
      \cong
      \widehat{\mathbf S}_{\mathrm{Tate}}(\mathfrak h),
    \]
    where the construction uses the effective
    \(\mathfrak{pgl}_N/\mathfrak{sl}_N\) Koszul model, omits the empty
    trace before taking block-sum primitives, applies the degreewise
    stable trace functor \(\operatorname{StTr}\) of
    Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category}\ref{adm:stable-trace-passage-functor},

    completion of Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-host-category}.  The

    Hochschild centre.
  \item The finite-\(N\) Hochschild target is
    \[
      C^\bullet_{\mathrm{Hoch},\mathrm{adm}}
      (A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N},A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N}).
    \]
    For \(\rho\in P\) and \(f\in\mathfrak h\), the assignments
    \[
      c^\rho\longmapsto\partial_\rho,
      \qquad
      u_f\longmapsto O_f=J(f),
      \qquad
      d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_f\longmapsto
      X_f=d_\pi O_f
    \]
    have coordinate degrees
    \(|c^\rho|=|\partial_\rho|=1\),
    \(|u_f|=|O_f|=0\), and
    \(|d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_f|=|X_f|=1\).  They are generator boundary
    values into this complex until an admissible Hochschild-centre

	  \item The stable coordinate Hochschild target is
	    \[
	      HH^\bullet_{\mathrm{adm},\mathrm{HKR}}
	      (A_{\partial,H}^{\mathrm{st}},A_{\partial,H}^{\mathrm{st}}),
	    \]
	    the Hochschild--Kostant--Rosenberg polyvector target of the

\begin{defn}[Trace-sector reduction]\label{defn:trace-sector-reduction}
  Let
  \[
    \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{neff}}
    =
    \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N
                 \oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N[1]),
    \qquad
    A^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\partial,N}
    =
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\bigl(\mathfrak{gl}_N,\,
       \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{neff}}\bigr),
    \qquad Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2],
  \]
  be the non-effective quotient-stack representative.  The effective
  Hamiltonian representative is
  \[
    \mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}}
    =
    \mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{gl}_N^{\oplus2}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}_N[1]),
    \qquad
    A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N}
    =
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}
      (\mathfrak{pgl}_N,\mathcal R_N^{\mathrm{eff}}).
  \]
  Thus the Hamiltonian scalar reduction is performed by the effective
  \(\mathfrak{pgl}_N/\mathfrak{sl}_N\) Koszul model, not by quotienting
  the algebra unit or the excess class \(\operatorname{Tr}\psi\).
  Let \(T_N\) be a target dg algebra or Hochschild cochain complex
  built from \(A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N}\) in
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).

  The primary source is the full row-finite coordinate realization of
  the continuous Chevalley--Eilenberg complex
  \[
    S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}
    :=
    \operatorname{Regr}_u
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE},\mathrm{coord}}
      \bigl(\mathfrak h\ltimes
        \mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}[1]\bigr).
  \]
  It is the product over finite graded-commutative coordinate words in
  \(c^\rho\), \(\rho\in\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}\), and
  \(u_f\), \(f\in\mathfrak h\), with the differential defined
  coefficientwise by the row-finite structure constants.  A
  trace-sector datum on \(T_N\) is a continuous cochain map
  \[
    \Phi_N\colon S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}\longrightarrow T_N
  \]
  whose coordinate values are
  \[
    c^\rho\longmapsto\partial_\rho,
    \qquad
    u_f\longmapsto O_f=J(f),
    \qquad
    d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_f\longmapsto
      X_f=d_\pi O_f.
  \]
  In the coordinate regrading,
  \(|c^\rho|=|\partial_\rho|=1\),
  \(|u_f|=|O_f|=0\), and
  \(|d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_f|=|X_f|=1\).

  Fix a Taylor coefficient bound \(W\) and a coordinate-word-length
  bound \(L\).  The vector space
  \[
    \mathfrak h^{\mathrm{Taylor}}_{\leq W}
    :=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{W+1}
  \]
  labels finitely many coefficients; it is not a Poisson or Lie
  quotient of \(\mathfrak h\).  Indeed,
  \(\{z_1,z_2^{W+1}\}=(W+1)z_2^W\), so
  \(\mathfrak m^{W+1}\) is not a Poisson ideal.  Let
  \[
    p_{W,L}\colon S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}
      \longrightarrow S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}
  \]
  be the continuous finite-rank idempotent whose image consists of
  coordinate words of length at most \(L\) whose monomial and
  principal-part labels lie in the finite coefficient range \(W\).  Thus
  \(S_{W,L}:=\operatorname{im}(p_{W,L})\) is a finite-dimensional
  coefficient space, not a Chevalley--Eilenberg complex of
  \(\mathfrak h^{\mathrm{Taylor}}_{\leq W}\).

  The target carries corresponding continuous finite-rank idempotents
  \(q_{N,W,L}\colon T_N\to T_N\).  We retain the notation
  \[
    \Phi_{N,W,L}:=q_{N,W,L}\Phi_N
  \]
  for the resulting finite output projection.  It is a coefficient map,
  not a cochain map with source \(S_{W,L}\).  Row-finiteness means that
  for every \((W,L)\) there is an enlarged finite pair
  \((W',L')\) such that
  \[
    p_{W,L}d_{\mathrm{CE}}
      =p_{W,L}d_{\mathrm{CE}}p_{W',L'},
    \qquad
    q_{N,W,L}\Phi_N
      =q_{N,W,L}\Phi_Np_{W',L'},
  \]
  and, for the target differential,
  \[
    q_{N,W,L}d_{T_N}
      =q_{N,W,L}d_{T_N}q_{N,W',L'}.
  \]
  These identities define the continuous differential and map on the
  full coefficient products.  They do not endow an individual Taylor
  projection with a Lie bracket or a differential.

  Trace evaluation in the coefficient range \(W\) is
  \[
    J_{\leq W}(f)
      =
      \bigl[
        \overline{\operatorname{Tr}\,\tilde f(\phi_1,\phi_2)}
      \bigr],
  \]
  where \(\tilde f\in\C\langle x,y\rangle_{\leq W}\) is any
  ordinary-word lift of the class
  \(f\in\mathfrak h^{\mathrm{Taylor}}_{\leq W}=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{W+1}\).  The
  completed \(J(f)\) is the inverse limit of these finite-window values
  after effective Hamiltonian reduction and stable trace passage.

  The finite-rank trace sector is declared when the full image is a
  strict admissible subcomplex of \(T_N\).  Equivalently, the induced
  coimage map
  \[
    S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}/\ker(\Phi_N)
    \longrightarrow T_N
  \]
  is a monomorphism in the chain category underlying
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\).  A failure of strictness or
  admissibility detected by the \((W,L)\)-coefficient projection is
  recorded as the
  image-admissibility obstruction
  \(\operatorname{Ob}^{\mathrm{im}}_{N,W,L}\); no trace sector in
  \(\mathcal B^{\mathrm{adm}}\) is declared until the obstruction is
  removed.

  When this admissibility condition holds, the finite-rank trace sector
  is the image/coimage subcomplex
  \[
    \operatorname{TrSec}_N(T_N)
    :=
    \operatorname{im}(\Phi_N)
    \cong
    S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}/\ker(\Phi_N)
    \subset T_N.
  \]
  Its finite coefficient output is denoted
  \[
    \operatorname{TrSec}_{W,L}(T_N)
    :=q_{N,W,L}\operatorname{TrSec}_N(T_N).
  \]
  This last object is a coefficient projection of the full subcomplex,
  not itself a finite-window subcomplex.  Thus the trace sector is a
  defined complex rather than a list of trace generators, while
  \((W,L)\) records only its continuous output coordinates.
  A bracketed or Hochschild trace sector further requires closure of
  this subcomplex under the restricted \(P_0\), cup, or Gerstenhaber
  operations used in the theorem; failure of this closure is recorded
  by the Hochschild-centrality obstruction complexes below.

  The stable admissible trace sector is
  \[
    \operatorname{TrSec}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{adm}}(T_\bullet)
    :=
    \widehat{\mathbf R\!\varprojlim}_{\,N}\,
    \operatorname{TrSec}_N(T_N),
  \]
  where the hat denotes completion in the coefficient topology defined
  by the projections \(q_{N,W,L}\), and the Roos complex is used for
  every non-strict inverse limit.  Its \((W,L)\)-coefficient is computed
  in the cofinal rank range \(N\geq W\), strengthened to
  \(N\geq\max(W,L+2)\) when a Loday--Quillen--Tsygan projection of
  length \(L\) is present; the individual coefficient spaces are not
  objects of an inverse system of cochain complexes.
  Procesi--Razmyslov controls the polynomial trace generators in the
  range \(N\geq W\), where \(W\) is total word degree after
  polarization.  The \(L+2\) term is the sufficient
  Loday--Quillen--Tsygan finite cochain-window bound used in
  Theorem~\ref{thm:stable-eulerian-lqt-window}; \(L\) counts
  Chevalley--Eilenberg length, not polynomial word degree.  The lower
  bound \(N\geq\max(W,L+2)\) is the cofinal rank contract for the LQT
  coefficient projection.  It is not an optimal finite-\(N\)
  trace-identity statement, and it is not a physical \(N\to\infty\)
  trace-state limit.

  For the coordinate polyvector target, the maps \(\Phi_{N,W,L}\) are
  the finite output projections of the full CE/PV coordinate
  isomorphism.
  For the Hochschild target
  \(T_N=C^\bullet_{\mathrm{Hoch},\mathrm{adm}}
  (A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N},A^{\mathrm{eff}}_{\partial,N})\),
  the full map exists only after an admissible Hochschild-centre
  Maurer--Cartan lift has been chosen.  Without that lift,
  \(c^\rho\mapsto\partial_\rho\),
  \(u_f\mapsto O_f=J(f)\), and
  \(d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_f\mapsto X_f=d_\pi O_f\) denote generator-level
  boundary values, not a Hochschild cochain map.

\begin{defn}[Source complexes for the global mixed-HT Deligne obstruction]\label{defn:global-deligne-source-complexes}

\begin{ex}[Finite-window admissible model]\label{exa:finite-window-admissible-model}
  Fix \(W<\infty\), and let
  \(V_N=\mathfrak{gl}_N\phi_1\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N\phi_2
  \oplus\mathfrak{gl}_N[1]\psi\).  Let
  \(I_N\subset\mathrm{Sym}(V_N)\) be the augmentation ideal for the
  total polynomial degree in \(\phi_1,\phi_2,\psi\).  The finite open
  Koszul window is
  \[
    \mathcal R_{N,\leq W}
    =
    C^\bullet_{\mathrm{CE}}\!\left(
      \mathfrak{gl}_N,\,
      \mathrm{Sym}(V_N)/I_N^{W+1}\right),
    \qquad
    Q\psi=[\phi_1,\phi_2]\bmod I_N^{W+1}.
  \]
  The derivation \(Q\) preserves \(I_N^{W+1}\), since it sends the
  degree-one generator \(\psi\) to the degree-two commutator.  This is
  a finite-dimensional non-effective open Koszul model.  Its effective
  Hamiltonian replacement uses \(\mathfrak{sl}_N[1]\psi\) and
  \(\mathfrak{pgl}_N\) as in
  Definition~\ref{defn:trace-sector-reduction}.  The spaces
  \[
    \mathfrak h^{\mathrm{Taylor}}_{\leq W}=\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^{W+1},\qquad
    \mathcal P_{\leq W}=\bigoplus_{1\leq a+b\leq W}\C\,\rho_{a,b},
  \]
  are finite coefficient/output spaces.  The Taylor space is not a Lie
  quotient, and these two spaces do not form a finite-dimensional
  Chevalley--Eilenberg source.  They define continuous projections of
  the full row-finite source
  \(S_{\mathrm{CE}}^{\mathrm{cont}}\).  On each such projection tensor
  products are algebraic, continuous duality is ordinary linear
  duality, and the projected Casimir
  \[
    C_{\leq W}=
    \sum_{1\leq a+b\leq W}z_1^az_2^b\otimes\rho_{a,b}
  \]
  is a finite sum.  For \(f\in\mathfrak h^{\mathrm{Taylor}}_{\leq W}\), choose an
  ordinary-word lift \(\tilde f\in\C\langle x,y\rangle_{\leq W}\).  The
  class
  \[
    J_{\leq W}(f)
    =
    \bigl[
      \overline{\operatorname{Tr}\,\tilde f(\phi_1,\phi_2)}
    \bigr]
  \]
  lies in
  \(H^0(\mathcal R_{N,\leq W}^{GL_N},Q)\).  The adjacent-transposition
  homotopy
  \[
    Q\operatorname{Tr}(\psi vu)
    =
    \operatorname{Tr}(u\phi_1\phi_2v)
    -\operatorname{Tr}(u\phi_2\phi_1v)
  \]
  remains inside \(\mathcal R_{N,\leq W}\) for words of length
  \(\leq W\).  Thus \(J_{\leq W}\) is a finite trace-evaluation
  coefficient of the full continuous trace-sector map; it is not a
  cochain map from a Taylor-quotient Chevalley--Eilenberg complex.  Its
  Hochschild realization is governed by the Hochschild-centre lift of
  Definition~\ref{defn:admissible-hochschild-centre-lift}.  The
  Procesi--Razmyslov stable trace identification in this window is used
  only for \(N\geq W\), or for \(N\geq\max(W,L+2)\) when a
  Chevalley--Eilenberg length \(L\) LQT projection is present.
\end{ex}

\begin{ex}[Non-admissible unweighted diagonal]\label{exa:unweighted-diagonal-nonadmissible}
  The naive infinite pair
  \[
    \mathfrak h_{\mathrm{naive}}=\prod_{a+b>0}\C\,z_1^az_2^b,\qquad
    \mathcal P_{\mathrm{alg}}=\bigoplus_{a+b>0}\C\,\rho_{a,b}
  \]
  with the product topology on \(\mathfrak h_{\mathrm{naive}}\) and the
  algebraic direct-sum topology on \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{alg}}\) is not
  an admissible object for the local theorem.  The partial sums
  \[
    C_M=\sum_{1\leq a+b\leq M}z_1^az_2^b\otimes\rho_{a,b}

  have no limit in

  direct-sum stage.  Thus the formal diagonal Casimir is not a kernel in
  this topology.  Any theorem using the Casimir must use a finite window,
  a Roos/pro-Matlis target, or a weighted Tate completion that supplies
  the missing convergence.
\end{ex}

\begin{thm}[Kinematic locality]
\label{thm:dict-kinematic-locality}
On the local pair \((X,L)\) with unimodularity datum
\(\omega=dz_1\wedge dz_2\) and reduced Hamiltonian Lie algebra
\(\mathfrak h=\C[[z_1,z_2]]/\C\cdot1\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] The equal-time bracket kernel \(\delta(t-t')\) on two
  brane-time points, smeared against test functions \(a(t),b(t')\),
  gives the support-local \(P_0\)-bracket
  \(\{B_f(a),B_g(b)\}=B_{\{f,g\}}(ab)\) with \(ab\) the pointwise
  product on \(\R_t\); disjoint supports bracket to zero.
\item[(ii)] Every topological translation
  \(v\in T\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\) is \(Q\)-exact, \(L_v=[Q,\iota_v]\),
  so \(H^\bullet(\mathrm{Obs}^{\mathrm{cl}})\) is locally constant
  along \(\R_t\) and inclusions of disks act by quasi-isomorphism.
\item[(iii)] Every antiholomorphic translation
  \(\partial/\partial\bar z_i\) is \(Q\)-exact,
  \(L_{\partial/\partial\bar z_i}=[Q,\iota_{\partial/\partial\bar z_i}]\),
  so the surviving \(\bar\partial\)-cohomology at the brane point is
  the formal jet algebra \(\mathfrak h\), Grothendieck-dually paired
  with
  \(\mathcal P=\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathrm{res}}(\C\cdot1)\subset
  H^2_{\mathfrak m}(\C[[z_1,z_2]])\,dz_1dz_2\).
\end{enumerate}
Items (ii) and (iii) hold on \(Q\)-cohomology; (i) is a chain-level
identity after distributional pairing.

Trace observables
\(\overline{\operatorname{Tr}\,f(\phi_1,\phi_2)}\) smeared as
\(B_f(a)\) satisfy (i)--(iii): support locality from the equal-time
bracket of the matrix-model fields, topological locality from the
\(Q\)-exact \(t\)-direction, formal-holomorphic locality from the
\(\bar\partial\)-exact transverse direction.  Shifted-cotangent
currents \(\Theta_\rho(B)\) satisfy (i) and (ii), and their
formal-holomorphic content is the residue current
\(\rho\in\mathcal P\) at the brane point.

The dg Lie algebra of an acceptable factorization algebra is therefore
locally constant in \(\R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\) and holomorphic in
\(B\subset\C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}\),
\[
  \Omega_c^\bullet(U)\widehat\otimes
  \Omega_c^{0,\bullet}(B)\widehat\otimes
  \mathfrak g,\qquad
  \mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\ltimes
  \mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}[1],
\]
with \(P_0\)-bracket on smeared currents fixed by (i).  This produces
the \(\C^2\) holomorphic factorization algebra
\(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\mathrm{hol}}\) and its mixed
topological-current enhancement
\(\mathcal F_{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\mathrm{mix}}\).
\end{thm}

action on \(\mathcal P\).  Beyond the Fourier--Rees theorem the spans of
\(\Psi_{a,b}\) and \(\rho_{a,b}\) carry distinct
\(\mathfrak h\)-module structures.

side: the closed-sector \(P_0\)-central operation on brane
observables.  Comparison morphism: \(\Phi_{\mathrm{coord}}\), the
coordinate dg CE/PV isomorphism on generators
\(c^I\mapsto\partial^I\), \(u_I\mapsto O_I\), and
\(d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_I\mapsto X_I=d_\pi O_I\); its restriction to a
bracket-admissible \(B\) is the \(P_0\) realization \(\Phi^B_{P_0}\).
  The parities are
  \[
    |c^I|=|\partial^I|=1,\qquad |u_I|=|O_I|=0,
  \]
  and the cotangent / Schouten bracket has degree \(-1\), with
  \([\partial^I,O_J]_S=\delta^I_J\).
  With \([H_I,H_J]=C^K_{IJ}H_K\), the exact differential table is
  \[
    d_{\mathrm{CE}}c^K=-\frac12 C^K_{IJ}c^Ic^J,\qquad
    d_{\mathrm{CE}}u_K=C^L_{KJ}u_Lc^J,
  \]
  and
  \[
    d_\pi\partial^K=-\frac12 C^K_{IJ}\partial^I\partial^J,\qquad
    X_K=d_\pi O_K=C^L_{KJ}O_L\partial^J.
  \]

\paragraph{Matlis principal-part labels.}
Algebraic side: the Grothendieck-dual of the formal jet algebra
\(\mathfrak h\), namely the residue/Matlis principal-part module
\(\mathcal P\cong\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}\).  Physical side:
the principal-part current observable \(\Theta_\rho(B)\) on a brane
interval.  Comparison morphism: the residue pairing
\(\mathfrak h\otimes\mathcal P\to\C\),
\(f\otimes\rho\mapsto\operatorname{Res}_0(f\rho)\), with Hamiltonian
coadjoint action on \(\mathcal P\) its image.  This is the
formal-holomorphic locality (iii) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:dict-kinematic-locality}.

As stated in the separation rule above,
\[
  \Psi_{a,b}\in\text{PBW special-fibre open homology},\qquad
  \rho_{a,b}\in
  \mathcal P\cong\mathfrak h^\vee_{\mathrm{cont}}
\]
live in different modules.  The only comparison morphism between them
is the Fourier--Rees label bridge \(\mathsf F_{\mathrm{Rees}}\) of
Theorem~\ref{thm:app-matlis-rees-fourier-bridge}, which acts on
associated-graded labels through Fourier-twisted polarization.  The
Hamiltonian coadjoint action remains the action on the Matlis module.
