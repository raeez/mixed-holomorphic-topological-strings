\chapter*{Hamiltonian fields and the trace sector}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Hamiltonian fields and the trace sector}

A Dirac brane probe on $\mathbb R^2_{\mathrm{top}}\times\mathbb C^2_{\mathrm{hol}}$ leads to a local comparison between Hamiltonian BF fields and boundary observables. Pointed Hamiltonian jets, shifted cotangent fields, and brane algebras on the formal symplectic plane give finite models for this problem. Pointed Hamiltonians vanish to second order and fix the chosen point. The affine Hamiltonian algebra also contains translations and is a separate input when the brane moves. The trace sector asks which Hamiltonian operations can be detected by invariant functions of matrix representations.

An ordinary trace retains the scalar dimension of a representation. Scalar reduction and a chosen augmentation remove that rank dependence and define compatible reduced traces. With the stated adic completions, the resulting stable map is a continuous homomorphism of commutative algebras. Lie and Poisson compatibility for full traces uses a separately specified bracket identity. Reduced Chevalley and polyvector operations must be compared on their actual carriers, with their differentials and topologies fixed.

The relative Rees comparison and finite formality calculations provide local algebraic inputs. A nonzero Poisson differential requires the corresponding twisted formality data, and a completed trace-sector comparison requires compatibility with the selected transition maps. Holomorphic chiral operations and mixed open--closed operations require their own operadic refinements. The global field-theoretic problem is to extend these local comparisons through descent and quantization while preserving the brane action, pairing, and anomaly data.

The physical bulk and the geometric centre are independently specified. A bulk action on a chosen collar must preserve the boundary operations and their higher compatibility maps. An equivalence criterion applies after that action is constructed. Cyclic amplitudes further require a trace domain, a pairing, and comparison maps for the two sewing geometries.
