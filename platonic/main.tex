\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Hamiltonian fields on a holomorphic symplectic plane act on matrix boundary observables. Their traces provide one comparison with bulk operations. Recovering the full boundary action also requires the Lie brackets, associative products, and the homotopies relating them. We develop finite pointed Hamiltonian models and specify the maps needed for their mixed holomorphic--topological realization.

The pointed Hamiltonians are formal functions vanishing to second order, so their vector fields fix the Darboux point. Their finite jet quotients are unimodular Lie algebras. The corresponding finite cotangent models satisfy the quantum master equation for the constant measure. Relative antisymmetrization compares their Rees enveloping cochains with the adjoint Chevalley complex over truncated and formal quantization coefficients. For quadratic Hamiltonians the decisive associative relation is
\[
 e\star f+f\star e+\frac12h\star h=-\frac38\hbarq^2,
 \qquad e=q^2/2,\quad f=-p^2/2,\quad h=-qp,
\]
where the Moyal product has Poisson normalization $\{q,p\}=1$.

These finite constructions distinguish the chosen Hamiltonian algebra from its scalar trace image. Their extension to continuous jets, chiral collisions, and physical bulk observables requires compatible coefficient maps, support conditions, and quantum operations. The open--closed comparison must also retain its cyclic pairing and sewing data.

\chapter*{Hamiltonian operations and boundary reconstruction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Hamiltonian operations and boundary reconstruction}
A Hamiltonian function determines a vector field through the symplectic form. At a matrix boundary it also determines an invariant trace. Comparing these two operations requires their actual brackets and differentials. A scalar trace by itself does not define an action on all boundary-changing operators.

The finite model begins at a fixed formal symplectic point. Hamiltonians in the square of its maximal ideal have vector fields that vanish there, and truncation by polynomial degree gives finite Lie algebras. Linear Hamiltonians instead move the point. They belong to the separately defined affine Hamiltonian algebra. The distinction matters when a brane position is allowed to vary.

The finite cotangent theory and its Rees cochains make the first comparison explicit. The oscillator relation above then shows that the associative algebra carries data beyond its Lie generators. Scalar reduction and an augmentation produce stable traces in a commutative target. Passing from these targets to a completed geometric centre requires a compatible action on modules, morphisms, and higher compositions.

For a physical mixed theory, define the bulk first from fields, an action, a BV pairing, boundary conditions, and a quantization on the chosen support domain. Define its boundary category and the object of permitted coherent local actions independently. The comparison problem asks for a map from bulk observables in a chosen collar to those boundary actions, preserving disjoint products, moving insertions, and collision operations. Source descent, target descent, and equivalence of the resulting objects are separate mathematical assertions.

Open--closed amplitudes require additional cyclic maps and pairings. Transverse closure and holomorphic sewing use different geometries and parameters. The matrix and finite Hamiltonian calculations provide inputs to these comparisons; the full quantum and global reconstruction requires their stated geometric and analytic extensions.

\chapter*{Notation and normalization}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Notation and normalization}
The ground field has characteristic zero.  Stable categories are presentable and idempotent-complete.  Tensor products preserve colimits separately.  Completions are separated inverse limits of finite weight or proper-height quotients.  The Igusa normalization is
\[
 \Dtheta=64\Dmon,\qquad \ChiTen=\Dmon^2,
\]
with $\Dmon$ monic.  Physical charge is additive; Gram degree is carried through its polarization cocycle.  Bar, factorization, and Hopf coproducts retain distinct notation and distinct geometric meanings.
