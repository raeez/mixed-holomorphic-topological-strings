\section{Admissibility criteria and obstruction complexes}\label{sec:open-obligations}

\providecommand{\Status}[1]{\par\noindent\textsc{Status:}\ \emph{#1}}
\providecommand{\Witness}[1]{\par\noindent\textsc{Witness location:}\ #1}
\providecommand{\Gate}[1]{\par\noindent\textsc{Gate for advance:}\ #1}
\providecommand{\Oblig}[1]{\par\noindent\textsc{Obligation (#1).}\ }

\paragraph*{Typology.}
Every entry below is one of three sharp objects.  A \emph{proved local
fixture} is a construction already used in the body and recalled here
for cross-reference.  An \emph{obstruction theorem} names the exact
cohomology class, cokernel functional, Roos primitive, or finite-window
matrix equation whose nonvanishing forbids the next theorem in a
specified habitat.  A \emph{construction problem} names the
mathematical ingredients --- habitat, kernel, propagator, primitive,
counterterm, transition matrix, or source-face datum --- whose
existence and explicit form would discharge the obligation.  Each
entry carries a \textsc{Status} tag and a \textsc{Witness location}
pointer to the load-bearing computation in the body or its
satellites.  The status values used are \emph{proved fixture},
\emph{partial} (the theorem closes on a certified subrange and an
exact obstruction is named for the remainder), \emph{closed
(no-go)} (an explicit obstruction class forbids the strict statement
in a specified habitat, while the theorem may admit a constructive
replacement in a changed habitat), \emph{open} (the obstruction
vector is named componentwise and at least one component lacks a
vanishing certificate), and \emph{external (target-data)} (the
obligation belongs to an external target theorem, not to the local
manuscript).

\paragraph*{Discipline.}
External compact Calabi--Yau, CoHA, quintic, OSV/GV, Abel--Jacobi, MNOP,
Igusa, BKM, and global BCOV material is not a core local obligation.  It
is a comparison surface only, and it cannot support the local QME, Ext,
cyclic, Weyl/Moyal, radial-parts, or kernel theorems without a separate
matched-conventions theorem and a vanishing obstruction vector.  An
admissibility downgrade --- replacing a strict bracketed identity by a
pro coordinate-window comparison, replacing a strict native current by
a one-pair analytic retract, or replacing a closed-exchange Costello
graph by a counterterm-only branch --- is admissible only when the
sharper formulation has been seriously attacked and the load-bearing
obstruction class has been named.  No entry below uses
``conditional'', ``expected'', or ``external input'' as a substitute
for a sharper construction problem or named obstruction class.

  \[

  \[

  ordinary cyclic boundary equation closes, and no signed obstruction row

  corrections with \(16\) and \(54\) terms respectively.  A nonzero
  correction is not a nonzero obstruction.

  \(\Omega^{\mathrm{rad}}_{a,b}=0\) for every bidegree.  The ordinary
  square-cell necklace cycle mechanism supplies
  \(\ker\partial=\operatorname{im}\partial_2\) in the relevant quotient,

  \[
    D^\square_{a,b}H_{a,b}=R^{\mathrm{free}}_{a,b},

  \]

  associated-graded Hodge homotopy on the decorated two-colour necklace

  \[
    [WXY]\longrightarrow[WYX].

  even a long one, is evidence for the mechanism but not the theorem.

  decorated harmonic projection
  \[
    \Pi^{\mathrm{harm}}_{a,b}R^{\mathrm{free}}_{a,b},
  \]

  \(\lambda_{a,b}\) with
  \(\lambda_{a,b}\mathcal C_{a,b}=0\) and

  \((\phi,-\lambda)\in\ker B_{a,b}^*\) with
  \[
    \lambda(E^+_{a,b})-\phi(T_{a,b})\ne0.

  \item \emph{Moyal and graph normalization.}

  \[
    \begin{aligned}
    f*g
    ={}&m\circ\exp\Bigl(\frac{\hbar}{2}
    (\partial_{z_1}\otimes\partial_{z_2}\\
    &\qquad\qquad
     -\partial_{z_2}\otimes\partial_{z_1})\Bigr)(f\otimes g).
    \end{aligned}

  \[
    \{f,g\}_\hbar
    =\sum_{s\ge0}

  any Costello realization must reproduce these coefficients after its
